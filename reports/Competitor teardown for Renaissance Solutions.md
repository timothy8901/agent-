# Renaissance Solutions can own AI's people side

*A competitive teardown of the 25 firms on RS's Linear "Competitor Research" board, following RS's competitive-teardown pipeline and adapted from three competitors to twenty-five. Evidence as of 2026-09-27.*

## Executive summary

**RS's clearest path to growth is to own the people-and-policy side of AI for small organizations in Chicago's northwest suburbs: helping staff and leaders decide how AI gets used, alongside whoever runs their technology.** Across the 25 competitors, that layer is either bundled into a tool or platform the seller also profits from (Integris, Thrive, HBS, TeamLogic, Masego), framed as a security discipline (Echelon, SideChannel, Concero), sold to the mid-market at prices small buyers can't reach (Sikich, Hartman, FRSecure, Freeman Clarke), or taught as tool training with no governance at all (New Horizons, ONLC). **No competitor rates Urgent; nine rate Medium** (Main & Machine, Masego, Sikich, TeamLogic, Integris, Echelon, Hartman, and, only in part, SideChannel for mid-size regulated prospects and New Horizons for RS's literacy line), and the closest match to RS's menu, Hartman, sells from Maryland to the mid-market. Three weaknesses run through the field and are RS's openings: **almost no independent proof** for AI work, **opaque pricing** at most of the firms that sell AI governance, and **size floors** RS sits beneath (Sikich's $5,000 minimum project, FRSecure's ~$48K-a-year vCISO, Thrive's estimated ~$160K per customer). The window is short: MSP roll-ups already bundle governance, and the vCISO notes expect security firms to add AI-governance offers within about a year [Estimate]. RS's constraint is that it is pre-revenue, with no pilot, placeholder prices and an unopened site, so its first moves must buy **proof and price evidence**, not scale.

**What RS should learn from them:** copy the mechanics that work: free self-checks (HBS, Masego, Cyvatar); paid diagnostics credited toward the next step (Main & Machine, NextGen); time-boxed, fixed-price packages (Fluent, vCISO.com); published prices with honest caveats (Purcell, FRSecure, LMT); founding-client offers traded for case studies (Masego, vCISO.com); and free education engines (FRSecure's cohort, TeamLogic's newsletter, LMT's library programs). Avoid what hurts them: hidden prices, jargon, anonymous proof, and governance tied to something they sell.

**Three pre-dge positioning statements to test** (each checked against all notes; none is used by a competitor):
1. **"The people side of AI, alongside your IT provider."** Your IT provider switches AI on; RS helps your people decide how to use it, and works with the MSP, never instead of it.
2. **"Every score shows its math."** Free readiness checks with no email required, visible scoring and results the client keeps. Every competitor's free check is a black box or a lead-capture form.
3. **"Bring one AI question. Leave with a written answer."** A fixed-price report and one-hour call on one decision, with no program attached. Every competitor's paid entry offer leads to a bigger sale.

**Three quick-win tactics for the next two weeks:**
1. **Run 2–3 founding-client pilots** of the two ready diagnostics (vendor AI due diligence; shadow AI discovery), free or deferred-payment until the bank account and insurance exist, in exchange for named case studies and price interviews.
2. **Open the doors deliberately:** confirm intake receipt, get the opening approved (REN-788), then send the first village letter and Curious Boomer issue featuring the free "show your math" check and a free Q&A.
3. **Pitch a "people side of AI" add-on to five NW-suburb MSPs**, starting with the owner-operated CMIT office in Hoffman Estates, aiming for one co-delivery pilot.

**Over 90 days:** price the six defined questions and productize a 30-day governance sprint; turn pilots into named stories and independent reviews; launch a free recurring "AI governance foundations" cohort and a syndicated brief for MSP partners; and watch the triggers that would raise a threat (for example, HBS buying a Chicago-area MSP, or Hartman entering the Midwest). **Caveat:** page fetching was blocked and the web-search budget ran out mid-research, so much competitor evidence is search-snippet level, and every legal, survey and local-channel lead remains to be verified.

## 2.2 Actionable strengths: eight mechanics the field has proven

1. **A free self-check at the top of the funnel.** HBS (5-minute readiness report), Masego (60-second score), Cyvatar (60-second scorecard), NextGen (5-minute quiz), Integris (gated AI-policy playbook). RS already has the forms; its edge is showing the math.
2. **A paid diagnostic credited toward the next step, with a deadline.** Main & Machine (100% credit within 60 days, capped at 25% of the sprint), NextGen (30 days), Speedwell (full credit), vCISO.com's $2,500 sprint "to test fit".
3. **Time-boxed, fixed-price packages.** Fluent's 30-day AI Sprint, vCISO.com's $24,000 90-Day Foundation, NeuralChainAI's 90-day pilot, Main & Machine's 90-day delivery guarantee.
4. **Published prices with honest caveats.** Purcell ("real projects typically run 1.5-3x the starting price"), FRSecure ("$4k–$6k or more" with an inclusions list and a "maintenance mode" step-down), vCISO.com (tiers with response SLAs), LMT's full menu, Concero's "from" prices.
5. **Proof-building offers.** Masego's "founding retainers from $500/mo" and vCISO.com's $2,500-a-month founding cohort traded for case-study rights; Main & Machine's rule to publish stories only with verified numbers and written sign-off; Echelon's named, title-attributed quotes.
6. **Education as a demand engine.** FRSecure's free CISSP Mentor Program (claimed 14 years, 60,000+ students), TeamLogic's monthly newsletter republished across franchise sites, Echelon's Speaker Series, HBS's "Tech On Tap" events, LMT's library cohorts.
7. **Named, staged journeys.** Thrive's crawl/walk/run, New Horizons' Align/Learn/Apply, ONLC's assessment-first AI Enablement Framework, Sikich's staged adoption framework.
8. **Retention mechanics.** Integris includes monthly AI Office Hours; Main & Machine sells a no-lock-in annual plan at 12 months for the price of 10 with refunds; SideChannel commits to a roadmap in 30 days and quarterly board reporting, but its 66.8% revenue retention shows month-to-month terms need a built-in cadence.

## 2.3 Weaknesses RS can exploit: proof, opacity, conflicts and size floors

- **A category-wide proof gap.** Among the direct AI competitors, the notes found **no independent reviews or attributed case studies for AI work**: one related-party case (Main & Machine), one unverifiable testimonial (NextGen), first-name quotes (Speedwell), none for any governed-AI boutique, none for the vCISOs or fractional CIOs, and none for the training firms. Only Sikich (Clutch 4.7 from 23) and FRSecure (Clutch 4.9 from 31) have independent review depth, for non-AI work. **The first local firm with named, verifiable AI governance outcomes wins on proof.**
- **Opacity.** MSPs (TeamLogic, CMIT, HBS, Integris's calculator, Thrive's unpriced credits), all three fractional CIOs, both national trainers, Echelon, Cyvatar and Masego's assessment all hide prices, while local MSPs (SafePoint IT, CelereTech) and several boutiques publish them.
- **Governance tied to what they sell.** Integris and Thrive make governance a feature of their own AI workspaces; HBS sells "establish governance" inside Copilot licensing and rollout; TeamLogic earns resale margin through Pax8; Masego is Claude-centric; Main & Machine's audit and Fluent's assessment feed their own builds; New Horizons is an authorized vendor partner.
- **Size floors RS sits beneath.** Sikich: $5,000+ minimum project and 80% mid-market clients; FRSecure: $5,000+ minimum and ~$48K a year for vCISO; HBS: ~$109K average revenue per customer [Estimate]; Thrive: ~$160K [Estimate]; SideChannel: 25-employee floor; Fluent: $10M+ revenue; Concero: 100–1,000 staff; Freeman Clarke: 20–40% of a CIO salary.
- **Technical or jargon-heavy framing.** Echelon's data poisoning and model theft, Sikich's "Orienteering Through the AI Journey", Cyvatar's "agentic", SideChannel's "zero-trust microsegmentation".
- **Distance.** None of the firms that sell AI governance as a named service to RS's buyers (Hartman, Concero, Masego, Echelon, SideChannel, Integris) documents a Chicago-area office. The locally present firms (TeamLogic, CMIT, Sikich, Thrive's Chicago office) sell governance as a technical or mid-market add-on.
- **Credibility failures at micro-competitors** (Speedwell's identity, NextGen's unnamed leaders, Concero's hidden team, Purcell's portfolio, LMT's contradictory figures) show how easily small AI firms lose trust. RS must be named, consistent and verifiable from day one.

## 2.4 Strategic opportunities: the people-and-policy layer, below the size floors, before the bolt-ons arrive

- **Own the people-and-policy layer beside the IT provider.** Local MSPs sell Copilot readiness and technical controls; none packages committee formation, staff literacy, acceptable use and intake workflows. That makes MSPs RS's most scalable channel rather than its enemy (HBS, TeamLogic, CMIT teardowns).
- **Serve the organizations below everyone's floor.** Villages, libraries, park districts, nonprofits, senior-living and aging-services organizations, and professional practices: the Priya persona's 120-person nonprofit, municipality or professional organization. Sikich's ~$6,500-a-month library client shows small public bodies do buy technology services, so the opening is specifically AI governance and literacy.
- **Build an education engine that feeds governance sales.** Combine FRSecure's recurring free cohort, LMT's library and workforce channels, SSG's named cohorts and New Horizons' paid 90-minute kickoff, all tied to governance deliverables none of them sells.
- **Move before the bolt-ons.** The vCISO notes expect ISO/IEC 42001 or AI acceptable-use add-ons from security vCISOs within about 12 months [Estimate]; MSP roll-ups already bundle governance; Hartman and Echelon already sell it to the mid-market. RS's window to own "AI governance for small organizations" locally is now.
- **Use partners for everything RS should not build.** MSPs (CMIT Elgin-Palatine, HBS Lisle, independent NW-suburb MSPs), security boutiques (vCISO.com, FRSecure), build shops (Purcell, Fluent, NeuralChainAI, Main & Machine), education peers (LMT, SSG), and small CPA, bookkeeping and law firms (the Sikich channel one size down).

### Statement 1: "The people side of AI, alongside your IT provider"

> *"Your IT provider can switch AI on. Renaissance Solutions helps your people decide how to use it: what staff are already doing, what's allowed, who says yes, and how everyone learns to use it safely. We work alongside your IT provider, never instead of it."*

- **Why it's open:** the MSPs (TeamLogic, CMIT, HBS, Integris, Thrive) sell governance as part of their own platforms and rollouts; the security firms frame it around threats; Sikich bundles it into audit and compliance relationships; Hartman sells it as mid-market advisory. The "people-and-policy layer" appears in the notes only as a recommendation for RS ([HBS Copilot services](https://www.hbs.net/news/hbs-earns-microsoft-365-copilot-specialization); [Integris Managed AI](https://integrisit.com/services/managed-ai/)).
- **Why it fits RS:** it matches the Priya persona's jobs (discover what employees do, set boundaries, educate "without frightening them", avoid "becoming permanent AI technical support") and turns MSPs into a channel ([REN-591](https://linear.app/renaissance-solutions/issue/REN-591/practice-with-priya-accidental-ai-champion-jim)). It also carries RS's glossary view that shadow AI is "not solved only by prohibition" into the first conversation.
- **Test:** use it as the opener in MSP partner calls and in the first village letter; measure whether MSP owners see it as complementary rather than competitive.
- **Watch-out:** a more pointed variant, "an independent check on what your provider's AI governance actually covers", is also unclaimed, but it could alienate MSP partners. Keep it for buyers without a partner relationship.

### Statement 2: "Every score shows its math"

> *"Check your organization's AI readiness for free, without giving us your email. Every answer shows how it moves your score, and the results are yours to keep, share or take to any provider."*

- **Why it's open:** every free check in the set is a black box or a lead-capture device: HBS's report, Masego's score, Cyvatar's scorecard (whose method isn't described), NextGen's quiz, ONLC's maturity assessment, FRSecure's unpublished "universal assessment methodology". None offers results with no contact details, and none offers portable output.
- **Why it fits RS:** it is already built. The Bespoke Forms engine's rubrics "calculate and explain numeric/category results", produce Markdown/PDF output, and need "No chatbot, account, contact details, or submission" ([REN-1062](https://linear.app/renaissance-solutions/issue/REN-1062/where-do-i-start-assessment-navigator-with-visible-landing-page)).
- **Test:** A/B the phrase on the free forms page and in newsletter calls to action; track completions and the share of completers who ask for the paid "report and follow-up".
- **Watch-out:** "no contact required" means fewer captured leads, so pair it with an optional "want a human to read this?" step, and never imply the score is a compliance finding.

### Statement 3: "Bring one AI question. Leave with a written answer."

> *"You don't need to know which service you need. Bring one decision, such as whether to renew an AI tool, what your team is already doing with AI, or what to teach your staff, and get a written recommendation and a one-hour follow-up call for a fixed price. No program, no retainer, nothing to build."*

- **Why it's open:** every paid entry offer in the set is designed to lead somewhere bigger: Main & Machine's audit credits toward a sprint, NextGen's assessment toward bundles, vCISO.com's sprint "to test fit" toward a retainer, Purcell's discovery toward a build, Fluent's sprint toward an agent, the fractional CIOs toward retainers, the trainers toward courses and credits. No competitor sells a single question as the unit of purchase or uses "you don't need to know what service you need."
- **Why it fits RS:** it is the lower paid tier Mike approved on 2026-09-25, with its question menu and the promise that "no continuing engagement is required" ([REN-1068](https://linear.app/renaissance-solutions/issue/REN-1068/expand-working-with-us-service-philosophy-assessment-options-and)), and it answers the category's opacity directly.
- **Test:** needs a price first (prices are still `[PRICE]` placeholders). Test it through the founding-client pilots in Part 4, then on the "Ways to start" page once the site opens.
- **Watch-out:** keep the question and review scope agreed before payment, as REN-1068 already requires, so "one question" doesn't become unbounded work.

**Runner-up to test later:** *"Governance you can outgrow"*: an interim AI rulebook sized for a 5–150-person organization, with a planned graduation path. No competitor uses the angle; it maps to RS's Interim Governance Installation and Graduation Checklist, which are still in Backlog.
