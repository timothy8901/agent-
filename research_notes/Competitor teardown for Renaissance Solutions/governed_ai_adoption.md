## 1. Company analysis: core business, problem solved, customer base, marketing and sales wins, SWOT

### Takeaway
All four are micro-to-small firms that sell AI adoption with a trust story. Only two sell governance artifacts outright:
- **Concero** publishes an AI Governance & Security package from $7,500 for firms with 100–1,000 employees, with a defense-supplier angle.
- **Masego**, a 13-year-old defense and GEOINT contractor, bundles acceptable-use rules, review gates and human-in-the-loop checks into a Claude Team rollout for small firms.

The other two sell trust in other ways. **NeuralChainAI** sells it through architecture: private, self-hosted AI by default. **Purcell** sells it through price transparency and client code ownership, and has no governance offer. **None of the four has independent reviews or named, attributable case studies for its AI adoption offer.**

### Inferences

**Customer-base estimates** (all [Estimate]):
- **Concero:** likely 0–10 AI engagements to date. Reasoning: no named clients or reviews, one listed employee on RocketReach (Section 2), and no press.
- **Masego:** a meaningful federal base, but likely fewer than 10 commercial AI clients. Reasoning: the "founding retainers" wording signals a newly opened practice, and there are no SMB references.
- **NeuralChainAI:** likely a few dozen clients at most, mostly technical ML/MLOps work from before its SMB repositioning.
- **Purcell:** likely a few dozen projects at most. At least one of the four homepage examples (QuantAIze) is probably internal.

**Personas** ([Estimate], based on offer design):
- **Concero:** COO, CIO or CISO at a B2B firm with 100–1,000 staff; IT or compliance leads at defense subcontractors.
- **Masego:** owner or managing partner and the office or finance manager at small law and accounting firms. The M365, QuickBooks, practice-management and payroll connectors point here.
- **NeuralChainAI:** CTO or IT lead at regulated SMB or mid-market firms (legal, healthcare, finance) worried about data leaving the building; US engineering leaders buying nearshore help.
- **Purcell:** ops or finance leads on NetSuite, HubSpot, Salesforce or Stripe who have outgrown spreadsheets; small professional-services firms.

## 6. RS lens: plays to copy, what to avoid, where RS wins, threat level, partner potential

### Takeaway
- **Masego is the closest analog to RS's commercial model:** a free score, then an assessment, then guardrails, training and a retainer, sold to small firms with a public $500/mo anchor. Its threat is **Medium**, mostly as a price anchor and funnel template, not as a local rival.
- **Concero is the closest analog to RS's packaging:** governance as a named, priced package with listed deliverables.
- **Purcell is the best model for price transparency and paid discovery.**
- **NeuralChainAI is the best model for a free session with a response-time promise and year-stamped decision content.**
- None of the four sells committee formation or chairing, incident tabletops, board briefings, quarterly governance reporting or vendor AI due diligence. That is RS's white space.

### Cited Findings (the anchors the plays rely on)
- **Concero:** assessment from $5K (2–4 weeks); governance and security from $7.5K (3–6 weeks); training from $3K per session; fractional advisory from $3K/mo; "not Fortune 500 complexity or SMB simplicity" — [homepage](https://conceroconsulting.com/) [Vendor claim]
- **Masego:** free ~60-second readiness score; "Founding retainers from $500/mo"; fixed-scope assessment and build; courses from Intro to AI to AI Governance; acceptable-use rules, review gates and human-in-the-loop — [AI consulting page](https://www.masegoinc.com/services/ai-consulting/) [Vendor claim]
- **NeuralChainAI:** free 30-minute session with a one-page scoping summary; proposal with cost ranges in 5 business days; 90-day sprint; flat retainer rather than per-seat pricing; 2026 decision guides — [homepage](https://neuralchainai.com/), [about](https://neuralchainai.com/about-us/), [Private AI page](https://neuralchainai.com/solutions/private-on-premise-self-hosted-ai/) [Vendor claim]
- **Purcell:** published starting prices and ranges; "real projects typically run 1.5-3x the starting price"; $3–8K paid discovery that produces written requirements and a fixed price; 30 days of included support; playbooks, glossary and guides; code shipped to the client's repository — [pricing](https://purcellanalytics.com/pricing/), [playbooks](https://purcellanalytics.com/playbooks/), [glossary](https://purcellanalytics.com/glossary/) [Vendor claim]

### Inferences (all [Estimate]: analyst recommendations for RS)

#### Concero → RS
**(a) Plays to copy or adapt**
1. **Show starting prices and durations on the homepage.** Once RS sets prices, it can list "from" prices with time boxes for its five packages: Readiness Assessment, Interim Governance Installation, Committee Formation & Training, Zero-to-Hero, and the retainer. Concero's figures ($5K, $7.5K, $3K per session, $3K/mo) are a ceiling reference for firms of 100–1,000 staff, above RS's smaller buyers.
2. **List every governance deliverable.** Concero spells out "policy, tool inventory, approval workflows, compliance documentation." RS's Interim Governance Installation page should list its artifacts: acceptable-use policy, AI tool register (the output of Shadow AI discovery), intake and approval workflow, incident-response plan, committee charter, and reporting template.
3. **Write a one-line size boundary.** Mirror Concero's "not Fortune 500 complexity or SMB simplicity" for RS's band, for example small businesses, nonprofits, villages, schools and professional offices, and treat Concero-type firms as the tier above.
4. **Use framework vocabulary without overclaiming.** Map RS's Bespoke Forms scoring to NIST AI RMF categories, so transparent scores rest on a recognized framework. Say "aligned to," not "certified."

**(b) What to avoid**
- Persuading with industry statistics instead of your own results.
- Hiding who is behind the firm.
- A brand name shared with many other firms.
- Offering "familiarity with" frameworks as proof.
- Stretching into agentic implementation, which blurs a governance-first message and creates a conflict of interest in assessments.

**(c) Where RS can win:** organizations under about 100 staff, below Concero's band; in-person delivery in Chicago's northwest suburbs, where Concero is a remote firm in Dulles; plain-language literacy for non-technical and older staff; visible, transparent scoring; vendor neutrality, since RS sells no builds.

**(d) Threat: Low.** Concero differs in geography, size band and federal focus, and shows no traction. It would only meet RS on Illinois manufacturers in the defense supply chain searching online.

**(e) Partner or referral potential: Low to moderate.** RS could refer defense-supply-chain clients with 100+ staff who need CMMC-adjacent AI governance. Concero could send RS in-person training or small-organization overflow in the Midwest.

#### Masego → RS
**(a) Plays to copy or adapt**
1. **Make a free readiness score the top of the funnel.** RS already owns Bespoke Forms with transparent scoring. Publish a free "AI Governance Readiness Score" that returns a scored one-page result and offers the paid Readiness Assessment. Masego shows the pattern is live in SMB AI, and RS's version can show how the score is built, which Masego's does not.
2. **Offer "founding client" pricing to buy proof.** Masego's "Founding retainers from $500/mo" honestly signals a new practice and lowers the entry bar. RS could offer a capped founding cohort, for example its first 5–10 organizations, at an introductory retainer in exchange for named case studies and testimonials. This directly fills RS's gaps in proof and willingness-to-pay data. Time-box it so the anchor doesn't stick.
3. **Sell a governed rollout of the tool the client already pays for.** Masego configures Claude Team (retention, data controls, SSO), adds connectors, writes acceptable-use rules, review gates and human-in-the-loop checks, then trains staff. RS can package a vendor-neutral version: a settings checklist for Copilot, ChatGPT, Claude or Gemini, plus an acceptable-use policy, review gates and staff training. Connector work goes to implementation partners.
4. **Publish a named course ladder.** Masego lists courses from Intro to AI to Building AI Agents and AI Governance, delivered as anything from a lunch-and-learn to a multi-week program. RS's Learn & Prepare menu can name its modules: beginner and "Curious Boomer" tracks, organization-wide literacy, committee training, and a tabletop exercise.

**(b) What to avoid**
- Running two unrelated businesses on one homepage; defense proof confuses SMB buyers.
- Tying the offer to one AI vendor.
- Hiding the assessment price behind "sized to your business."
- An open-ended low retainer floor that anchors RS below a sustainable price.

**(c) Where RS can win:** vendor-neutral governance (Masego is Claude-centric); in-person presence in Illinois; governance depth that Masego doesn't list (committee formation and chairing, incident tabletops, board briefings, quarterly reporting, vendor due diligence); nonprofit, municipal and older-learner audiences.

**(d) Threat: Medium.** Masego is the nearest match to RS's menu (assessment, guardrails, training, retainer). It sells remotely to small firms nationwide, with a public $500/mo anchor and a free score that RS prospects may find online. Offsetting this: there is no evidence of Illinois presence or SMB traction, and its brand is defense-first.

**(e) Partner or referral potential: Moderate.** Masego could handle RS's Claude implementation referrals (connectors, secure deployment) while RS does the neutral governance, committee and literacy work. Masego's SDVOSB and 8(a) status could open federal subcontracting that RS couldn't reach alone.

#### NeuralChainAI → RS
**(a) Plays to copy or adapt**
1. **Offer a free session with a deliverable and a response-time promise.** NeuralChainAI promises a one-page scoping summary or ROI map, then a written proposal with real cost ranges within 5 business days. RS could offer a free 30-minute "AI risk snapshot" call, a one-page summary, and a proposal within 5 business days.
2. **Write year-stamped buyer decision guides.** Examples: "Self-Hosted Private AI vs ChatGPT Enterprise… (2026)" and "How to Run an LLM Locally: A 2026 Business Guide." RS equivalents could be "Copilot vs ChatGPT Team vs Claude Team: which data settings to turn on (2026)" and "Should our village staff use ChatGPT? A 2026 policy starter." These double as education-wedge articles.
3. **Time-box a flagship.** The 90-day sprint maps onto RS's "Zero-to-Hero" as a 90-day program with milestones: assessment, policy, committee, training, first quarterly report.
4. **Promise predictable cost.** NeuralChainAI sells "one-time deployment plus a flat retainer" instead of per-seat pricing. RS can price its retainer per organization, not per head.

**(b) What to avoid:** a service list of more than 11 items; anonymous statistics and unattributed testimonials; quote-only pricing; a brand that reads as crypto; an unclear home base.

**(c) Where RS can win:** local presence in the US and Illinois; governance and the people side (policy, committees, literacy) rather than infrastructure; non-technical buyers; neutrality, since RS doesn't need clients to host models.

**(d) Threat: Low.** NeuralChainAI is based in Canada, sells to technical buyers, and is infrastructure-heavy. It overlaps with RS only on fractional AI leadership and privacy messaging.

**(e) Partner or referral potential: Moderate.** When an RS governance review rules out public SaaS for a legal, healthcare or finance client, NeuralChainAI is a plausible private-AI implementer. It could refer policy, training and committee work back.

#### Purcell Analytics → RS
**(a) Plays to copy or adapt**
1. **Radical price transparency.** For each package, publish the starting price, typical range, duration and payment schedule, and add an honest FAQ line such as "most engagements land at X–Y because…". Purcell's "1.5-3x" candor is a trust device in itself.
2. **Paid discovery that ends in a fixed price.** Purcell's discovery takes 1–2 weeks, costs $3–8K, and produces written requirements and a fixed-price proposal. RS can position its Readiness Assessment the same way: as paid discovery that ends in a fixed-price Interim Governance Installation proposal, optionally credited if the client goes ahead.
3. **Included post-launch support, then an optional retainer.** Purcell includes 30 days. RS could include 30 days of support after installation, then convert to the retainer subscription.
4. **Build a content moat and state ownership plainly.** Purcell's industry playbooks, glossary and buyer guides suggest RS versions: sector governance playbooks (nonprofits, local government, medical and dental offices, accounting firms) that support sector policy adaptation, and a plain-language AI governance glossary. Mirror Purcell's "code shipped to your repo" with "every policy, form and template is yours to edit; no license."

**(b) What to avoid:** listing internal projects as client work; a brand name that implies a person who isn't the operator; having no testimonials; service sprawl into unrelated lines like SEO.

**(c) Where RS can win:** Purcell sells no governance, training or policy, so any organization adopting AI through Purcell still needs RS-type governance. RS is local and friendly to non-technical buyers.

**(d) Threat: Low.** Purcell is tangential: a build shop.

**(e) Partner or referral potential: Moderate to high.** Transparent fixed-price builds from $8K make Purcell a clean referral when an RS assessment recommends automation or dashboards. In turn, Purcell's AI-integration clients need governance. Remote delivery makes geography irrelevant.
