## 1. Company analysis: core business, problem solved, customer base, marketing and sales wins, SWOT

### Takeaway
The three firms use three different models to sell fractional security leadership to smaller organizations:
- **vCISO.com** is a founder-led boutique in Pittsburgh with fully published, productized prices. Its "founding cohort" offer (a discount in exchange for testimonials) points to a very young practice with no public proof.
- **SideChannel** is a small public company (OTCQB: SDCH) that started in Worcester, MA. It bundles a bench of former CISOs with its own RealCISO GRC software and Enclave zero-trust platform. It is the only one of the three that explicitly lists "AI governance advisory."
- **Cyvatar** is a VC-backed managed-security provider in Irvine, CA ($12M raised in 2020–21). It includes its "Agentic vCISO" free inside an unpriced remediation subscription and claims 229 customers.

### Inferences
- **vCISO.com is very likely in its first year or so of selling in its current form** [Estimate]. It is offering half-price retainers to get its first testimonials and case studies, it has no reviews, and the founder personally leads every engagement. REN-246's "Evidence unclear" label still fits.
- **SideChannel probably has about 40–60 vCISO clients** [Estimate]:
  - Trailing-twelve-month revenue is about $6.9M (see section 2).
  - In Q3 FY2026, vCISO revenue fell below half of the roughly $1.8M quarter, so it is under about $3.6M a year.
  - At a typical $6K–$7K/mo (the midpoint of its published $3K–$12K range), that works out to roughly 40–55 clients.
  - Enclave and RealCISO customer counts are separate and unknown.
- **Cyvatar's "229 customers" probably counts every customer since 2019, not current customers** [Estimate]. The claim is paired with "zero major breaches," which reads as a track-record statistic.
- **REN-260's "Location unverified" label can now be resolved:** Cyvatar is in Irvine, CA (sources in section 2).

**vCISO.com SWOT**
- Strengths:
  - an exact-match category domain
  - published prices with tiered response SLAs (48 hours, 24 hours, same day)
  - a productized, low-risk entry offer (the $2,500 Sprint)
  - hands-on offensive-security credentials and in-house pentesting
  - month-to-month terms and a fast start
- Weaknesses:
  - key-person dependency and a capacity ceiling, since the founder leads every engagement
  - no public proof at all
  - tier names and prices that differ between pages (see section 6)
  - a narrow tech-startup niche
- Opportunities [Estimate]:
  - an easy bolt-on of AI governance (ISO/IEC 42001, AI acceptable-use policies), since policy authoring and governance are already in scope
  - CMMC Level 2 demand
  - an on-site niche in Pennsylvania
  - referral ties with auditors
- Threats:
  - a crowded field that publishes prices and pricing guides — [Zip Security](https://www.zipsec.com/blog/vciso-pricing), [Rhymetec](https://rhymetec.com/vciso-pricing-breakdown/), [BD Emerson](https://www.bdemerson.com/article/vciso-cost), [vCSO.ai benchmark](https://vcso.ai/learn/vciso-pricing-benchmark-2026/), [FractionalCXO](https://fractionalcxo.to/guides/vciso-cost-guide), [Essendis](https://www.essendis.com/post/vciso-cost-virtual-ciso-pricing), [Inventive HQ](https://inventivehq.com/services/vciso)
  - MSPs delivering vCISO through platforms such as [Cynomi](https://cynomi.com/learn/vciso-costs/)
  - price compression toward the $3K/mo floor [Estimate]

**SideChannel SWOT**
- Strengths:
  - public-filer transparency and about $7.4M in revenue
  - a bench of former CISOs, with named backups
  - broad framework coverage, including SEC cyber disclosure and GLBA
  - its own software, which lifts gross margin (54.9% in Q3 FY2026)
  - a named CIO testimonial (Panduit)
  - an explicit AI governance advisory line
- Weaknesses:
  - shrinking revenue (down 7.7% over nine months of FY2026)
  - continuing net losses and a very thin reported cash balance
  - trailing-twelve-month revenue retention of only 66.8%
  - an OTCQB micro-cap listing after a 1-for-52 reverse split
  - management attention split between services and product
- Opportunities:
  - an MSP/MSSP channel for Enclave and RealCISO
  - a stated margin potential of 64–75% (section 3)
  - selling AI governance advisory to existing vCISO clients [Estimate]
  - regulatory demand from CMMC and SEC cyber disclosure [Estimate]
- Threats:
  - execution risk and dependence on MSP partners, both flagged in earnings coverage (section 3)
  - well-funded zero-trust and GRC competitors [Estimate]
  - financing and dilution risk [Estimate]
  - low-price entrants: vCISO.com's $2,500/mo cohort rate, and Cyvatar including its vCISO for free

**Cyvatar SWOT**
- Strengths:
  - execution plus vCISO in one subscription
  - a recognizable partner stack, including Booz Allen for incident response
  - an outcome narrative and a 229-customer claim
  - free lead magnets
  - founder pedigree: Corey White (ex-Cylance) and Craig Goodwin (ex-Fujitsu), per section 2
  - VC backing and Inc. 5000 growth
- Weaknesses:
  - no public pricing
  - absolute outcome claims that can't be verified
  - no independent reviews
  - a small team, per data aggregators
  - an unexplained legal entity, "Cyvatar AI Holdings LLC" (section 2)
  - no dated news found after August 2024
  - dependence on margins from third-party tools
- Opportunities:
  - licensing the Agentic vCISO to MSP partners. The page's phrase "increasing close rates" reads like partner-facing copy [Estimate].
  - CMMC demand
  - displacing security-ratings tools for SMBs
  - adding AI-policy templates to its 54-policy library [Estimate]
- Threats [Estimate]:
  - MDR and tool vendors moving down-market
  - scrutiny of "agentic" claims
  - runway, since no funding round has been disclosed since 2021
  - supplier changes within its partner stack
