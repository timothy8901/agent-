## 1. Company analysis: core business, problem solved, customer base, marketing and sales wins, SWOT

### Takeaway
FRSecure (Edina, MN; founded 2008) is a mature, product-agnostic security consultancy. It sells vCISO, risk assessment, testing, incident response and compliance mainly to mid-market and small businesses. It grows through reputation (Clutch 4.9/5 from 31 reviews) and a free-education engine: a free CISSP Mentor Program running for 14+ years, a weekly podcast and free templates. Echelon Risk + Cyber (Pittsburgh, PA; founded c. 2020–21) is younger and growing faster. It bundles vCISO leadership with hands-on implementation, managed security and, since May 2025, AI governance for regulated mid-market and brand-name clients. It markets through syndicated press releases (Inc. 5000 and service launches), named testimonials, events and a speaker series.

### Inferences
- **Personas.** FRSecure's buyers look like CTOs, IT directors and security administrators at $10M–$1B companies, plus compliance owners who need PCI, CMMC or SOC 2 evidence. This is inferred from the Clutch mix, the on-site quote titles and the compliance-heavy menu cited above. Echelon's named buyers skew more senior and more regulated: CRO at a bank, ISO at a construction firm, InfoSec manager at a global consumer brand, VP of IT at an NBA team.
- **Active client estimate [Estimate].** For FRSecure, 2,500 is a cumulative, undated vendor claim. Estimated revenue of about $15–24M (section 2) divided by a typical annual spend of $20–40K (the Clutch mode is $10–50K; vCISO is $48–72K/yr) gives roughly **400–1,200 active clients a year**. For Echelon, estimated revenue of about $14–22M divided by an assumed $50–150K average annual spend (mid-market STaaS/MSSP/testing mix with brand-name clients) gives roughly **100–400 active clients**. Both ranges are wide, and neither firm publishes a figure.
- **FRSecure SWOT.**
  - *Strengths:* 18 years of operating history. Clutch 4.9/5 across 31 reviews. Vendor neutrality. A broad bench, including a PCI QSA and CMMC. Published vCISO pricing. A very large free education community (a claimed 60,000+ students) and recurring owned media. In-house vulnerability research (a CVE in 2026). All cited above.
  - *Weaknesses:* Founder Evan Francen, whose book and persona seeded the podcast brand, stepped down as CEO in January 2024 (section 2). Headcount looks flat at about 100 (section 2). Glassdoor compensation and benefits rate 3.4/5 and career opportunities 3.7/5 (section 5). No dedicated AI governance SKU was found (section 6). The vCISO floor of about $48K/yr prices out very small organizations. Cost (4.7) is its lowest Clutch sub-score.
  - *Opportunities:* Add AI-governance and AI-risk modules to the vCISO and risk-assessment products. Its 2026 content already covers AI agent risk and AI vulnerability scanning. CMMC demand should return once the timeline firms up. The mentor-program community could be monetized with advanced paid courses or referral partnerships.
  - *Threats:* The CMMC delay is squeezing compliance revenue; FRSecure's own Episode 261 covers "CMMC Delay and Compliance Costs." Faster-growing rivals bundle MSSP and AI governance (Echelon). Compliance-automation platforms and their partner networks are commoditizing readiness work (Echelon's Drata listing shows the channel).
- **Echelon SWOT.**
  - *Strengths:* Two consecutive Inc. 5000 listings with 285% three-year growth. A full-stack "strategy → implementation → 24/7 operations" model. Marquee references. A live AI governance line mapped to ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF. Strong Glassdoor sub-ratings: career opportunities 4.3, compensation 4.2. A growing partner bench plus a CSO hire. All cited above or in sections 2 and 5.
  - *Weaknesses:* A young firm (c. 2020–21). No public pricing. No independent client-review profile found. One negative culture review on Glassdoor. Web hygiene gaps (the STaaS URL returned 404). Heavy reliance on self-published PR over independent validation.
  - *Opportunities:* AI governance demand. CMMC 2.0. Recurring MSSP revenue. Regulated verticals it already references: banking, health care, automotive, manufacturing and construction. National expansion.
  - *Threats:* Its rank fell from No. 433 (2025) to No. 1,228 (2026), which points to slowing percentage growth. MSSP is a crowded, capital- and talent-intensive market. Slippage in the CMMC timeline. Retaining talent while scaling.

## 7. RS lens: plays to copy, what to avoid, where RS can win, threat level, partner potential

The most transferable lessons are FRSecure's **free, recurring education cohort as a brand and pipeline engine** and its **published "from" pricing with inclusions**, and Echelon's **named proof about follow-through, syndicated milestone PR and a standards-mapped but plain-English AI governance story**. ### Inferences

**(a) Plays RS could copy or adapt**
1. **A free, recurring cohort as the top of the funnel (from FRSecure's CISSP Mentor Program).**
   - *What FRSecure does:* the same named program every year, practitioner-taught, evening sessions (13 × 2 hours in 2026), on-demand recordings, LMS registration, a Discord community, and staff and alumni LinkedIn amplification. Continuity has compounded into a claimed 60,000+ students over 14+ years.
   - *RS adaptation:* a free "AI Governance Foundations" cohort each spring and fall: 6–8 weekly 60–90-minute evening sessions, recorded, delivered online plus one in-person session in the Mount Prospect area. It would run two tracks: (i) org leaders and would-be committee members, feeding Committee Formation & Training and the Readiness Assessment; (ii) a "Curious Boomer" confidence track, feeding the newsletter and training.
   - *Funnel design:* registration through a Bespoke Forms intake that includes a short AI-readiness self-score with transparent scoring, so RS can segment and follow up. At the end, offer a certificate and a template kit (the CISSP "carrot" equivalent) and invite qualifying organizations to a paid Readiness Assessment.
   - *What to fix versus FRSecure:* instrument conversion from day one, because FRSecure publishes no pipeline metrics.
2. **Publish a "from" price with inclusions and a step-down (from FRSecure's vCISO FAQ).**
   - *What FRSecure does:* states "$4k–$6k or more per month," lists what's included (assessments, roadmap, scanning, consulting, portal) and says costs fall in "maintenance mode."
   - *RS adaptation:* publish "from" prices for the Readiness Assessment and the retainer subscription, with an explicit inclusions list: quarterly governance reporting, a set number of new-tool reviews, regulatory monitoring and policy maintenance. Add a stated step-down after Interim Governance Installation, and deliver status through a client portal or Bespoke Forms dashboards.
   - *Benchmark:* FRSecure's vCISO (~$48–72K+/yr) and Clutch spend data ($3.9K–$60K/yr; mode $10–50K) give a willingness-to-pay ceiling for adjacent security programs. A narrower AI-governance retainer for small organizations should anchor well below the $4K/month vCISO floor. This is inference; RS has no WTP data yet.
3. **Named, title-attributed proof about follow-through, plus syndicated milestone PR (from Echelon).**
   - *What Echelon does:* homepage quotes with names and titles that stress execution ("didn't just tell us what was wrong, they made sure it got fixed"). It syndicates every milestone (Inc. listing, service launch) through a wire so it shows up on Globe and Mail, Barchart and OpenPR.
   - *RS adaptation:* ask the first 3–5 pilot clients for named quotes that stress follow-through, such as "they installed it, not just recommended it," which supports Interim Governance Installation. Push them to independent profiles early (Clutch or Google), because FRSecure's 31 Clutch reviews are a durable asset and Echelon has none. Announce the Readiness Assessment launch and the free cohort with a low-cost wire release plus village newsletters and local papers.
4. **Standards-mapped but plain-English AI governance, plus a speaker-series menu (from Echelon).**
   - *What Echelon does:* aligns its assessment to ISO/IEC 42001 and NIST AI RMF. It argues the CISO "can't own this alone," and it offers a Speaker Series to InfoSec chapters, internal teams and local events.
   - *RS adaptation:* map each Readiness Assessment domain and score to NIST AI RMF and ISO/IEC 42001 in an appendix for credibility, but deliver the findings in plain language. Publish a 4–6-talk speaker menu (Shadow AI, vendor AI due diligence, AI literacy for staff, board briefing) for chambers of commerce, Rotary, libraries, village boards, SHRM and nonprofit associations, and local ISACA and IIA chapters.

**(b) What RS should avoid**
- **Selling AI governance as a technical security sub-discipline.** Echelon's list of data poisoning, model theft, adversarial inputs and prompt injection suits CISO-led, regulated buyers, but it will read as jargon to RS's small-organization audience.
- **Opaque, call-us pricing (Echelon).** It conflicts with RS's transparency ethos and slows small-organization buyers.
- **Service sprawl into capital- and talent-heavy lines** (24/7 MSSP, pen testing, CMMC assessment). Echelon runs these with about 86 people and a partner bench. RS should refer these out.
- **A free program with no designed next step.** FRSecure's stated rationale is workforce development, and its conversion is undocumented. RS cannot afford a program that builds goodwill without pipeline.
- **Tying the brand only to the founder.** FRSecure's podcast and book brand began with Evan Francen, who stepped down as CEO in 2024, and the podcast now has other hosts. RS should give its cohort, newsletter and assessment their own names so they can outlive any one voice.
- **Relying only on vendor-hosted testimonials (Echelon).** Collect independent reviews as well.

**(c) Where RS can win against them**
- **Below their price floor.** The targets are sub-$10M businesses, nonprofits, municipalities and villages, and professional practices. FRSecure's vCISO starts at about $48K/yr with a $5K+ minimum project, and Echelon's named clients are mid-market and brand-name organizations.
- **AI governance as the core offer, not an add-on.** FRSecure has no AI governance SKU in captured sources. Echelon's AI governance is one line inside Risk Advisory + GRC, framed around threats. RS's full menu (Shadow AI discovery, vendor AI due diligence, intake and approval workflows, quarterly reporting, regulatory monitoring) is broader on the governance-operations side.
- **Whole-organization literacy, including non-technical and older staff.** FRSecure's free education targets security professionals (CISSP), and Echelon's talks target InfoSec audiences. Neither sells org-wide AI literacy or a "Curious Boomer"-style confidence track, and REN-269 found that wedge thin overall.
- **Local, in-person facilitation in NW Cook County.** Governance committee formation, incident tabletops and board briefings work best in person, and neither firm shows a Chicago-area presence in captured sources.
- **Transparent scoring.** Bespoke Forms can show clients exactly how the readiness score is computed. That contrasts with FRSecure's homepage claim of a NIST-based "universal assessment methodology" (REN-261), whose scoring isn't published in captured sources, and with Echelon's unpublished approach.
