## 1. Company analysis: core business, problem solved, customer base, marketing and sales wins, SWOT

### Takeaway
- **Integris** is a five-year-old roll-up of company-owned MSPs, formed in October 2021. After closing First Focus on 2026-09-01 it has 1,200+ staff and 4,000+ customers across the US, Australia and New Zealand. It leads its marketing with "Managed AI" backed by an ISO/IEC 42001 certificate, plus a free AI-policy playbook.
- **Thrive** is the larger and older platform: about $400M revenue in 2025, 1,600 staff and 2,500+ customers. It targets mid-market and "sophisticated SMB" buyers and sells security-heavy managed services plus a Managed AI Workspace that works with many AI models. Its showcase proof is enterprise-grade logos, not SMB volume.

### Inferences
- **[Estimate] Revenue per customer.**
  - Thrive: about $400M ÷ 2,500 customers ≈ $160K a year.
  - Integris: an estimated $180–300M (see §2) ÷ 4,000 ≈ $45–75K a year.
  - So Thrive's book leans mid-market and Integris's leans SMB. Both averages are far above what a first-time RS buyer would spend.
- **[Estimate] Integris US-only customers: about 2,300–3,000 (low confidence).** This scales the 4,000+ combined figure by an assumed US staff share of 700–900 out of 1,200+ (see §2).
- **Likely buyer personas** (inferred from industries and testimonials):
  - Integris: owner or managing partner, CFO/COO, compliance officer at a community bank or credit union, office manager, and the internal IT lead in co-managed deals.
  - Thrive: CTO, CIO or Head of IT, and CFO at mid-market organizations in financial services, healthcare, professional services and the public sector.
- **AI is a subscription add-on at both.** Each sells AI as a "Managed AI Workspace" attached to managed IT. Governance is therefore packaged as a platform feature, not a standalone advisory engagement.

### Inferences
- **[Estimate] Integris revenue: about $180–300M** for the combined group, annualized after First Focus. Low confidence; treat it as order of magnitude.
  - Method: 1,200+ staff × $150K–$250K revenue per employee.
  - The upper bound is Thrive's own ratio (about $400M ÷ 1,600 ≈ $250K).
  - The lower bound is a conservative assumption for SMB-heavy MSPs, not sourced.
  - US-only, assuming 700–900 staff: about $105–225M.
- **[Estimate] Integris US headcount: 700–900.** The floor is 600+ (December 2024), plus TechMD (its largest deal) and organic hiring. The cap is the 1,200+ combined figure, which includes First Focus.
- **[Estimate] Integris growth.** Staff roughly doubled from 600+ (December 2024) to 1,200+ (September 2026), mostly through acquisitions (TechMD, First Focus). The organic growth rate is not public.
- **[Estimate] Thrive 2026 revenue run-rate: about $430–500M.** That is the $400M 2025 base, plus a full year of the five 2025 acquisitions, plus organic growth.
  - Reaching $1B in 4–5 years implies about 20–26% compound annual growth: (1,000/400)^(1/5) − 1 ≈ 20%, and (1,000/400)^(1/4) − 1 ≈ 26%.
  - That pace requires continued M&A.
- **[Estimate] Thrive headcount in 2026: about 1,650–1,800.** That is 1,600 (September 2025) plus Worksighted (November 2025, size not public).
- **Likely organization model (inference).** Both follow a PE "buy-and-build" pattern:
  - Central shared services: NOC/SOC/help desk, finance, marketing and M&A.
  - Regional client-facing teams inherited from acquired firms.
  - A vCIO/vCISO advisory layer to grow accounts.
  - Add-on deals financed through the sponsors (lenders not public).
  - Integris now also has an APAC region (First Focus: Australia, New Zealand, Philippines).
- **Leadership signals (inference).**
  - Integris handed the CEO role over in 2025 and reversed it in 2026, then stressed a "seamless customer experience." That hints the board wanted the founders to fix post-merger integration or service-quality problems.
  - Thrive's CEO change came right after the Berkshire deal (January then February 2025). New capital followed by a new operator is a common pattern.

### Gaps
- Debt facilities, lenders and leverage for both firms: none surfaced, and the search budget ran out.

## 6. RS lens: plays to copy, what to avoid, where RS can win, threat level, partner potential

### Takeaway
- **Governance is already being sold, but bundled.** Both roll-ups show that SMB and mid-market buyers are being sold "AI governance" today, but as a feature inside a managed-AI subscription.
- **What to copy.** RS should borrow their funnel: a free AI-policy asset leading to an assessment, included office hours, a named maturity path, and trust anchored in standards.
- **How to position.** RS should present itself as the independent, vendor-neutral governance layer focused on people (committees, boards, training). They cannot play that role because of how they are built.
- **Threat:** Integris medium, Thrive low.

### Cited Findings (the facts the plays build on)
- **Integris' lead magnet:** a gated "AI policy playbook" ([Vendor claim](https://integrisit.com/lp/ai-policy-playbook/)), led by the stat "77% of organizations don't have written policies for governing AI tool use" ([Vendor claim](https://integrisit.com/services/managed-ai/)).
- **Integris' packaging:** it offers a customizable AI Acceptable Use Policy (whether it costs extra was not confirmed), and every Managed AI Workspace subscription includes monthly AI Office Hours — [Vendor claim](https://integrisit.com/services/managed-ai/).
- **Thrive's maturity path:** a crawl/walk/run AI Adoption Model combining "advisory-led alignment, guardrails, training, and managed delivery" — [Vendor claim](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/03/10/3252838/0/en/thrive-expands-managed-ai-services-with-model-agnostic-ai-workspace.html); [Vendor claim, Linear](https://thrivenextgen.com/services/ai/).
- **Neither publishes prices.** Integris offers only a calculator or estimate; Thrive bills AI in credits without prices — [Vendor claim, Linear](https://integrisit.com/services/managed-it/pricing/); [Vendor claim, Linear](https://thrivenextgen.com/thrive-expands-managed-ai-services-with-model-agnostic-ai-workspace/).
- **ISO/IEC 42001 certification** typically takes 4–9 months and costs about $20K–$60K (Stage 1 and Stage 2 audits, three-year validity, annual surveillance audits). The standard was published in December 2023 — [Independent, compliance-vendor blog](https://www.konfirmity.com/blog/iso-42001); [Independent](https://www.iso.org/standard/42001).

### Inferences

#### (a) Plays RS could copy or adapt
1. **Free policy asset leading to a paid assessment (entry offer and content).**
   - Integris gates an AI-policy playbook behind a stat hook.
   - RS version: a Bespoke Forms "AI Policy Gap Check" of 10–15 questions with transparent scoring. It returns a one-page gap summary and invites the Readiness Assessment.
   - Promote it through village newsletters, the Curious Boomer newsletter and articles.
   - Build RS's own local stat from aggregated, anonymized results (e.g. "X of Y NW-suburban organizations we checked had no written AI policy") instead of borrowing a national number.
2. **Included office hours to keep clients (packaging and pricing structure).**
   - Integris includes monthly AI Office Hours in every subscription.
   - RS version: make a monthly group "Governance Office Hours" the anchor benefit of the retainer, alongside quarterly governance reporting. One session serves many small clients, so the extra cost per client is low.
3. **A named maturity path (packaging).**
   - Thrive's crawl/walk/run AI Adoption Model turns a menu into a journey.
   - RS version: brand "Zero-to-Hero" as three or four stages mapped to its goal groups (Understand & Decide → Learn & Prepare → Build & Improve → Communicate & Connect). Give each stage a fixed-fee entry offer and clear exit criteria.
   - Publish the diagram in articles and proposals.
4. **Trust anchored in standards, without the certificate (proof).**
   - Integris uses ISO/IEC 42001 as its trust signal. RS cannot justify a $20K–$60K certification now.
   - RS can instead publish a plain-language crosswalk showing how its governance installation maps to ISO/IEC 42001 and the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, and add an "ISO 42001 readiness" tier later.
   - Say "aligned to," never "certified."

#### (b) What RS should avoid
- **Tying governance to a tool RS sells.** Both roll-ups make governance a feature of their own workspace; RS's value is its independence.
- **Opaque pricing** (calculators, credits). Once willingness to pay is tested, publish "starting at" prices for the Readiness Assessment and entry workshops. Small buyers value predictability.
- **Unverifiable superlatives** ("#1," "fastest growing") and loose certification language.
- **Competing on 24/7 operations, security tooling or breadth,** where RS cannot win.
- **Stacking offers before one package has proven demand.** The roll-ups' leadership churn and employee reviews show the cost of growing without integrating.

#### (c) Where RS can win
- **Independent second opinion.**
  - Vendor AI due diligence that evaluates MSP-provided "Managed AI Workspaces": data handling, logging, model access, what the provider's ISO 42001 scope actually covers, and exit terms.
  - Shadow AI discovery outside any one vendor's workspace.
  - A provider's ISO/IEC 42001 certificate covers its own AI management system, not the client's governance.
- **Governance of people, not platforms.** Committee formation and chairing, board and executive briefings, incident tabletop exercises, and organization-wide literacy training. The MSPs' "training" centers on adopting their own platform.
- **Below their economic floor.** Micro and small organizations, villages and other local public bodies, nonprofits, and older learners. For these buyers the roll-ups' estimated average relationship of $45K–$160K a year is irrelevant, and plain language matters.
- **Local, founder-led speed and continuity,** against roll-ups with CEO churn and mixed employee sentiment.
- **Fixed-fee engagements with no lock-in,** against subscription commitments.

### Inferences
- **Read the demand signal carefully.**
  - Two PE-backed platforms are spending on AI-governance positioning. Integris holds a 42001 certificate that typically costs $20K–$60K and 4–9 months; Thrive built a governance-branded workspace.
  - That shows sophisticated sellers expect SMB and mid-market buyers to respond to "AI governance" language.
  - It is evidence from the supply side only. Neither discloses how many customers bought Managed AI, so it does not prove anyone will pay for standalone governance.
- **The category is converging.**
  - Both named their product "Managed AI Workspace." A governed workspace spanning many AI models is becoming a standard MSP product, and basic AI-policy work is being folded into it.
  - RS should not sell a standalone basic policy. It should sell what sits above any workspace: the decision process, the committee, the evidence, training and reporting.
- **Language to borrow:** "written AI policy," "AI Acceptable Use Policy," "guardrails," "governed environment," "crawl/walk/run," "from AI curiosity to production," "office hours."
- **Language to counter: "backed by ISO 42001."** An MSP's certificate covers the MSP's own AI management system. It does not cover the client's governance or its board's oversight duties.
- **MSP governance messaging is sharpest in regulated sectors:** for Integris, community banks, credit unions, law and wealth management; for Thrive, financial services, healthcare and government. When RS adapts policies by sector, it should pick sectors it can reach locally and where its independence counts.

#### REN-1030 view (from the Integris and Thrive side)
Integris and Thrive are distinct firms and should keep separate issues, but they do not merit equal depth. Integris merits continued individual tracking at light, quarterly depth, because it is the most direct public benchmark for RS's flagship offer: an SMB-focused MSP selling AI governance with an ISO/IEC 42001 claim, a gated AI-policy playbook, a customizable Acceptable Use Policy and included monthly office hours. Its trajectory is also volatile: a CEO change in 2025, the founders back in March 2026, and an international deal closed in September 2026. Thrive can sit in a grouped "PE-backed MSP platform" benchmark alongside Integris, with trigger-based rechecks instead of full re-research. Reopen Thrive only if it announces an ISO/IEC 42001 certification, publishes AI-governance pricing or an SMB governance package, buys a Chicago or NW-suburban MSP, or starts running local AI events from its Chicago office. What Thrive contributes uniquely is evidence on packaging (the named crawl/walk/run adoption model, a workspace spanning many AI models, GRC via Abacode) and proof that mid-market buyers are being sold governed AI. Both differ in structure from TeamLogic IT and CMIT. They are company-owned roll-ups whose national messaging is centralized and uniform, whereas the franchise brands' offers vary by local office. One shared "PE platforms" benchmark note can therefore cover both roll-ups, while the franchise brands are better tracked only where a local office actually competes in RS's area; I did not check in this pass whether TeamLogic or CMIT has such an office.
