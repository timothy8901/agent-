## 1. Company analysis: core business, problem solved, customer base, marketing and sales wins, SWOT

### Takeaway
New Horizons and ONLC are national IT-training houses. Both have added AI enablement (tool training, prompt workshops, assessments, leadership sessions) to long-running Microsoft/Office catalogs and sell it to organizations by quote only. Learn More Technologies (LMT) and Stronger Systems Group (SSG) are founder-led micro-practices selling plain-language AI confidence to specific communities (adults 50+; women leaders) through community venues. Only LMT publishes prices, and none of the four shows named, independent customer proof.

### Inferences
**SWOT: New Horizons**
- *Strengths*: national brand and reach (claims 100K+ trained a year and a 35-country network); authorized vendor partnerships; a full ladder from a 90-minute kickoff to multi-day training and consulting; the credit program locks in budget early; sister Educate 360 brands supply change management, project management and Six Sigma add-ons.
- *Weaknesses*: organization pricing is hidden; proof figures are unattributed; reputation carries franchise-era baggage (Trustpilot 2.6/5 from only 5 reviews; reported legal actions against specific franchisees, per [REN-249](https://linear.app/renaissance-solutions/issue/REN-249/new-horizons)); the offer is tool-centric with no policy or governance product; integration load from folding United Training into the brand in 2023.
- *Opportunities*: the Copilot/Microsoft 365 adoption wave (AI now attached to every Office course); a government buyer path; consulting upsell; departmental SMB pilots.
- *Threats*: free vendor training and cheap e-learning; prompt training becoming a commodity; AI tools reducing demand for instructor-led training; private-equity cost pressure or an exit; governance boutiques that own the "what is allowed" question.

**SWOT: ONLC**
- *Strengths*: 40+ years under the same founders; a large public catalog in exactly the Office/Excel tools where AI features now live; flexible delivery (center, remote, on-demand); a coherent assessment-first AI framework; SparkLabs' NDA and anonymization options address data-privacy worries.
- *Weaknesses*: corporate AI is quote-only; public proof is weak (first-name testimonials); independent B2B reviews are thin; aggregators disagree on its headquarters; no visible governance or policy offer.
- *Opportunities*: SMB and mid-market Copilot adoption; assessments converting into larger programs; the public class calendar as a lead source.
- *Threats*: New Horizons and other national trainers; free Microsoft and LinkedIn content; founder succession or a sale.

**SWOT: Learn More Technologies**
- *Strengths*: a clear niche (adults 50+) and empathetic voice; published prices; a free-forever funnel; WIOA eligibility; a library partnership spanning 6 branches; MBE certification (useful in supplier-diversity procurement); the founder's AARP credibility.
- *Weaknesses*: micro-scale and founder dependency; self-reported figures that contradict each other; scattered SKUs (brand advisory for marketers, a creative gallery, keynotes); no independent reviews; proof is concentrated in Texas.
- *Opportunities*: AgeTech and an aging workforce; library and senior-center programming budgets; employer age-inclusion efforts; licensing through train-the-trainer.
- *Threats*: free nonprofit alternatives such as OATS/Senior Planet from AARP; low-price consumer courses such as That Clicked at $67 lifetime (both per [REN-269](https://linear.app/renaissance-solutions/issue/REN-269/fringe-scan-2026-09-02)); swings in public workforce funding.

**SWOT: Stronger Systems Group**
- *Strengths*: credible practitioner background (Head of IT, Director of IT) plus a master's in computing in education; a distinctive identity program (StrongHER Systems™); pairs AI literacy with systems thinking; a New York community network (a nonprofit client, an outside community-organization host).
- *Weaknesses*: solo and possibly part-time; no pricing; no reviews; mixed SKUs (resume writing); the "AI Strategy & Consulting" site title conflicts with the education positioning; little visible scale.
- *Opportunities*: women's leadership groups, sororities, nonprofits and employee resource groups; board AI literacy.
- *Threats*: New York boutiques such as INTLDA that pair literacy with governance sprints (per [REN-269](https://linear.app/renaissance-solutions/issue/REN-269/fringe-scan-2026-09-02)); larger trainers; limited capacity.

## 2. Financial and operational: funding and ownership, revenue, headcount and hires, organization model

### Takeaway
Only New Horizons has a capital story. Since 1 Oct 2023 it has been a brand of Educate 360, which Morgan Stanley Capital Partners has owned since June 2019. Educate 360 bought New Horizons from Camden Partners, and New Horizons was once a NASDAQ-listed company. ONLC appears founder-owned and self-funded since 1983; LMT and SSG are bootstrapped founder micro-firms. No company has a reliable public revenue or headcount figure, and the aggregator numbers for Educate 360 conflict and look wrong.

## 3. Market position: top competitors, strategic direction and roadmap, recent pivots

### Takeaway
New Horizons and ONLC compete head-on with each other and with other national IT trainers for "AI tool adoption" budgets. Both are moving from course sellers to "AI enablement program" designers: New Horizons attaches Copilot to every Office class, and ONLC leads with an assessment and labs. LMT and SSG operate in a thinly occupied niche (RS's own scan found the plain-language, systems-thinking confidence space "thin, not crowded").
