## 1. Sikich — Company analysis (core business, problem solved, customers, marketing and sales wins, SWOT)

### Takeaway
Sikich is a Chicago-HQ professional-services firm with CPA roots and about 2,500 staff. It calls itself a "consulting, technology, and compliance platform." It sells mid-market buyers ($10M–$1B revenue, 80% of its Clutch client mix) one relationship that covers:
- audit and tax
- ERP/CRM
- managed IT and vCIO
- cybersecurity
- a packaged AI practice: the Sikich AI Solutions Studio® plus a virtual Chief AI Officer (vCAIO)

Its marketing runs on ranking badges, a steady trade-press PR cadence, a very large insights library and named, trademarked AI frameworks.

### Inferences
**SWOT** (analyst synthesis; supporting facts cited in sections 1–5)

*Strengths:*
- CFOs already trust their CPA firm, which gives Sikich a natural path to sell technology and AI into those relationships.
- Breadth: audit, compliance, ERP, managed IT, SOC and AI from one firm.
- Scale: about 2,500 staff and $438.9M FY24 net revenue.
- Bain capital behind it.
- Packaged AI governance offers have been in market since late 2024.
- Chicago HQ.
- Strong customer rating: Clutch 4.7.

*Weaknesses:*
- Cost is its weakest Clutch score (4.2). One reviewer reported rate increases and asked for clearer project pricing.
- Combining finance and IT roles creates conflict-of-interest risk; one documented client exit came from a finance-team conflict.
- Employee compensation and benefits rating is low (Glassdoor 2.8, down 11% in a year), which risks staff churn on accounts.
- Frameworks read as enterprise jargon.
- Small business is only 10% of the Clutch mix.

*Opportunities:*
- Upselling AI governance to mid-market clients who already buy audit and IT from Sikich.
- Jefferson Wells adds risk-and-compliance capacity (plus interim staffing and executive search), a natural home for AI controls work.
- Burwood adds enterprise IT consulting.
- The Basis partnership adds AI-enabled delivery margin.
- Offshore/APAC delivery.

*Threats:*
- AI eroding billable accounting hours.
- Integration risk from five deals in about 10 months.
- Larger Chicago CPA/consulting rivals.
- Eventual return expectations from PE capital.

**Other inferences**
- The problem Sikich solves is "one trusted adviser" for finance-adjacent technology and compliance. A mid-market leader can buy AI strategy and governance from the same firm that audits them.
- Estimated client count, [Estimate]: roughly 7,000–20,000 active clients. This divides FY24 net revenue ($438.9M) by an assumed $20K–$60K average annual revenue per client. The low end reflects small tax and audit engagements; the high end reflects the $72K–$150K/yr managed IT and ERP retainers seen on Clutch. Low confidence.
- RS's "under-served buyer" idea needs nuance. Sikich already sells managed IT to at least one north-suburban public library (about $6,500/month). Small public bodies do buy from Sikich, so RS's opening is specific to AI governance, not IT in general.

### Gaps
- No official client count, no campaign metrics (leads, conversion), and no AI-governance case study with outcomes. The Data & AI quote is undated and not about governance.
- AI Studio and vCAIO pricing is not published; there is no rate card (REN-244).
- AI framework wording relies on trade-press restatements because the pages themselves could not be opened.

## 6. Sikich — RS lens (plays to copy, what to avoid, where RS wins, threat level, partner potential)

### Inferences
**(a) Plays RS could copy or adapt**
1. **Fractional executive retainer** (packaging and pricing structure; adapted from the vCAIO). Bundle four RS services into one named monthly role, sold as the retainer subscription:
   - standing committee chairing
   - ongoing tool review
   - regulatory monitoring and policy maintenance
   - quarterly governance reporting

   Unlike Sikich, give it a plain-language name and publish its scope and price.
2. **Opportunity plus risk in the entry offer** (entry offer). Sikich's Opportunity Assessment pairs use-case and ROI discovery with gaps in "talent, technology, and data governance." RS's flagship Readiness Assessment should add an "opportunities worth pursuing safely" section, so buyers see upside as well as risk. That makes it easier to justify paying.
3. **The CPA-trust channel, one size down** (channel). Sikich's engine is CPA trust carried over into technology and AI. RS can copy it by partnering with small local CPA, bookkeeping and law firms in the NW suburbs that cannot build an AI practice. RS would be their AI governance and literacy arm, with a referral or white-label arrangement.
4. **A named, staged journey plus proof that shows spend** (content and proof). Sikich's nine-step framework is the spine of its content. RS can make its four goal groups the visible journey (Understand & Decide → Learn & Prepare → Build & Improve → Communicate & Connect), with each step linking to a fixed-scope package and "Zero-to-Hero" as the whole path. RS should also collect verified reviews (Clutch or Google) from early clients, including project size, as Sikich's Clutch reviews do. This offsets RS's current lack of demand evidence.

**(b) What RS should avoid**
- Opaque or escalating pricing, Sikich's weakest score.
- Enterprise jargon such as "Orienteering Through the AI Journey" with small-organization buyers.
- Tying governance advice to implementation or resale revenue. That invites the conflict-of-interest perception seen in one Sikich client exit.
- Chasing $50M+ companies whose CPA firm is already selling them AI governance.
- Competing on breadth (ERP, SOC, managed IT).

**(c) Where RS can win**

- **Non-technical audiences.** Boards and staff who need literacy and confidence: older learners, volunteers, library and village staff.
- **Vendor-neutral policy work.**
- **Locality.** Sikich's listed Chicago-area offices are Chicago and Naperville; RS is in the Mount Prospect / NW Cook area and has village newsletter channels.
- **Principal-led continuity,** in contrast to employee-churn signals at larger firms.
- **Committee formation and training.** Neither firm visibly markets it; this is absence of evidence from this pass, not proof.

**(d) Threat level to RS: Medium.**
- Why it matters: same AI governance vocabulary, Chicago presence, strong trust with CFOs, and an AI Education Program that overlaps RS's literacy offer.
- Why it is not higher: a different size band and much higher price points.
- It rises to **High** if RS moves up to $20M+ regulated mid-market firms, or if Sikich launches a low-cost, productized AI governance package for small organizations or nonprofits.

**(e) Partner or referral potential**
- Subcontracting: **Low**. Sikich has in-house AI governance, vCAIO and education.
- Outbound referrals from RS to Sikich: **Moderate**. RS can refer audit, tax, ERP, managed IT and SOC needs, which builds goodwill.
- Inbound referrals of very small organizations Sikich cannot serve profitably: speculative and untested.

## 12. HBS — RS lens (plays to copy, what to avoid, where RS wins, threat level, partner potential)

### Takeaway
HBS is a low direct threat but the better partner candidate of the two. Its AI line is technical and Microsoft-led. That leaves open the people-and-policy layer RS sells: literacy, committees, acceptable use and intake workflows.

HBS's free five-minute AI Readiness Assessment is a lead-magnet pattern RS can copy with its Bespoke Forms engine and transparent scoring. **Threat: Low.**

### Cited Findings
- HBS's Copilot pitch is "assess readiness, align licensing, establish governance and roll out Copilot." [Vendor claim] — [HBS](https://www.hbs.net/news/hbs-earns-microsoft-365-copilot-specialization)
- Its free five-minute AI Readiness Assessment returns an individualized report with next steps. [Vendor claim; search-summary only] — [HBS](https://www.hbs.net/blog/how-to-prepare-for-ai)
- The vCISO handles cyber-insurance and vendor-compliance questionnaires. [Vendor claim] — [HBS vCISO](https://www.hbs.net/services/vciso)
- HBS runs informal "Tech On Tap" events on AI and Copilot. [Vendor claim] — [HBS](https://www.hbs.net/events-and-webinars/tech-on-tap-ai-copilot)
- HBS's Illinois office is in Lisle. [Vendor claim] — [HBS Illinois](https://www.hbs.net/locations/illinois)
- HBS inherited an SMB services practice from Computer Solutions (2018) and publishes SMB Copilot content. [Vendor claim] — [HBS](https://www.hbs.net/News/HBS-expanding-service-offering); [HBS blog](https://www.hbs.net/blog/microsoft-365-copilot-business-smb)

### Inferences
**(a) Plays RS could copy or adapt**
1. **Free self-check → personalized report → paid next step** (entry offer). Build it on Bespoke Forms, but show the scoring rubric, which differentiates RS from HBS's black-box report. Route scores straight to the paid Readiness Assessment.
2. **Questionnaire-driven packaging** (packaging; adapted from the vCISO). Create a fixed-scope "AI questions from customers, insurers and auditors" kit: an AI use inventory (Shadow AI discovery), a policy, and standard answers. This packages RS's vendor due diligence and policy services around a pain buyers already feel.
3. **Informal local events** (channel and content). Adapt "Tech On Tap" into RS-hosted library or village sessions ("AI on Tap"), promoted through village newsletters and the Curious Boomer newsletter.
4. **Copilot-rollout companion** (channel and partner). Offer a vendor-neutral "before you switch on Copilot/ChatGPT" governance and training pack that sits alongside whichever MSP handles the technical rollout.

**(b) What RS should avoid**
- Competing on technical implementation: Copilot licensing, Azure development, digital twins, SOC.
- Vendor-tied recommendations.
- Free assessments with no clear paid path. HBS can afford one because it has implementation services to sell afterwards; RS's paid next step must be explicit.
- Relying on badges instead of customer proof.

**(c) Where RS can win**
- Small organizations and public bodies that don't need an integrator averaging about $109K a year per customer.
- Non-technical governance and plain-language training.
- NW-suburb locality (HBS's only Illinois office is in Lisle).
- Independent delivery that clients can review publicly.

**(d) Threat level to RS: Low today.**
- It rises to **Medium** if HBS buys a Chicago-area MSP, or productizes AI governance and literacy for SMBs as part of Copilot bundles.

**(e) Partner or referral potential: Moderate to High (the best of the two firms).**
- Co-deliver the people-and-policy layer for HBS's Illinois Copilot and AI clients.
- Refer both ways: RS sends managed IT, security and vCISO work to HBS; HBS sends literacy, committee and policy work at clients too small for its consulting.
- Approach through the Lisle office or HBS's Microsoft practice.
- Risk: PE-owned platforms may prefer to build this in-house or buy it.

### Gaps
- Whether HBS already sells AI literacy training, acceptable-use policy or committee services (not found in this pass).
